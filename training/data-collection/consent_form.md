## What the study is about

OpenMeet helps interviewers notice when an answer may be read from a screen
or written by an AI tool. It gives the interviewer a *signal* to ask a
follow-up question, never a verdict. To find out how often these signals
are right and wrong, we need recordings of people answering interview
questions in different ways, some of them on purpose by reading or
paraphrasing a prepared answer.

## What you will do

A video call of about 40 minutes in your browser. You answer 15 interview
questions (technical, open and about your experience). For some answers we
ask you to answer normally; for others to read a script you wrote, to read
an answer an AI tool wrote, or to put an AI answer into your own words.
There are no right or wrong answers and nothing is graded.

## What is saved, and what is not

For each answer, the interviewer's computer saves:

- the question and a transcript of your answer, with the time of each word;
- a series of eye positions (two numbers per video frame, ten a second),
  computed on that computer from your video;
- the scores of the checks, and which condition the answer was in.

**Not saved:** audio, video, pictures of you, your name. The saved data is
stored under your participant id. The link between your name and your id
is on this form only, which is kept separately.

## How the data is used

The data is used to measure and tune the signals (for example, how often an
honest answer is flagged), and only aggregate numbers are published (in the
project report and its data card). The data is kept in the team's private
